# How an engagement runs

One engagement, start to finish, in the order things actually happen.

## 1. Intake — agree on the job before doing it

The client states the problem and hands over whatever material they have. The consultant writes a brief: the question, the scope, the material relied on, and what a finished answer looks like. A separate review checks the brief for completeness and for claims it cannot support. The client approves the brief — that approval is the launch act. If the brief is rejected or redirected, the diagnosis re-runs; a brief is never quietly patched.

## 2. Launch — open the workspace

Launch creates the engagement folder, mints its number, writes the registry row, and starts the first leg. From here on, every stage's output is a file in that folder.

## 3. Legs — do the work in stages

A typical engagement runs these stages (the exact set depends on the job):

- **Investigate** — gather and label the evidence. Each item is marked as data or strategy, and tied to its source.
- **Draft** — write the answer: findings, reasoning, and recommendations.
- **Review** — a fresh session grades the draft against the brief: what was read, what was skipped, every required field, every number's source.
- **Decide** — the client (or the consultant, where the decision is his) rules on the open questions. Rulings are filed, not remembered.

## 4. Gates — pass, or go back with findings

After a stage ends, its gate runs. Pass moves the engagement forward. Fail returns named findings, and the stage runs again with those findings in view. A second failure on the same gate stops the engagement and goes to the client's desk with the record — it does not loop forever.

## 5. Close — leave a record, not a memory

Closing writes three things: the decisions made, the deliverables, and the open items. The folder stays as the archive. If the same client returns, the next engagement starts by reading the prior record — so nobody has to re-explain, or re-decide, what was already settled.

## Why this shape

Consulting fails in predictable ways: the brief drifts, the draft never gets a hostile read, numbers go unchecked, decisions evaporate. Each part of the machine exists to close one of those failure modes. The sample engagement in `sample-engagement/` shows the whole run on a fictional job.
